Bakery Ingredient Sentinel

A concept demo for a central bakery that supplies its cafés. It looks at two issues raised during my visit to Tatte's Old Colony Ave bakery: occasional raspberry shortages that end in emergency grocery runs, and the challenge of assigning drivers across four delivery routes.

Sample data only. Every quantity, recipe amount, lead time, shelf life and route time below is made up for illustration. None of it is Tatte data. Café addresses on the Routes tab come from public listings and may be out of date; the four-route split is my own illustration, not Tatte's actual routes.

    Route comparison

    Some routes are short on miles but slow at every stop; others are mostly highway. Both can feel unfair for different reasons.

    One option: a 4-week rotation

    If the gap comes from the routes themselves, rotating them spreads the hard ones evenly. Each driver's 4-week average becomes the same.

    Before changing anything: ask the drivers

    This model only guesses at workload. The drivers know what actually makes a route hard.

    • Which stops take longest, and why (parking, docks, stairs, waiting for staff)?
    • Which delivery windows are hardest to hit?
    • How do start times and traffic affect each route?
    • Is the issue the hours, the physical load, the vehicle, or something else?
    • What would make the assignment feel fair?

    A few weeks of route logs (start/finish times, stops completed, on-time arrivals) would turn this sketch into real numbers.
